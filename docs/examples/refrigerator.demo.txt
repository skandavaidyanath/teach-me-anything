      - b1 There is no cold to make: A fridge adds no cold; it carries heat out of the box, which is why the coils on its back feel warm.
      - b2 Evaporating fluid drinks heat: A liquid that boils absorbs heat from its surroundings, so refrigerant boiling at minus twenty five degrees in the inside coils pulls heat out of the food.
      - b3 Squeeze it and it gets hot: The compressor squeezes the cold refrigerant gas until it is hotter than the kitchen, so heat can flow out of it.
      - b4 Dump the heat, then let it expand: The hot gas gives its heat to the room in the back coils and turns back into liquid, then an expansion valve drops its pressure so it is cold again.
      - b5 Why an open fridge heats the kitchen: The fridge dumps the heat it moves plus the compressor's own work, so leaving the door open warms the room overall.
