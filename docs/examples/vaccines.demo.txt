      - b1 A vaccine contains no working germ: Most people think a vaccine fights the disease, but it holds no live, working virus and does nothing to any germ; on day one it changes nothing you could measure, yet weeks later you are protected.
      - b2 The first meeting is slow: When a new virus arrives, the few immune cells that happen to fit its shape take about ten to fourteen days to find it, multiply, and make enough antibodies, while the virus doubles every few hours.
      - b3 Show the shape, skip the danger: A vaccine shows the immune system only the virus's surface shape, such as the spike protein, so the matching cells are found and multiplied with no infection doing damage in the meantime.
      - b4 Memory cells stay behind: After the response fades, a much larger standing population of memory cells that fit that shape remains, so the starting count for the next encounter is hundreds of times higher.
      - b5 The second meeting is a race you win: On real exposure, memory cells respond in about two to three days instead of two weeks, and antibodies rise faster and higher, stopping the virus before it multiplies enough to make you sick.
      - b6 Back to the shot of nothing: A vaccine is a rehearsal, not a weapon: it shifts the starting line of the race, which is why boosters top up memory and why a virus that changes its shape can partly slip past it.
