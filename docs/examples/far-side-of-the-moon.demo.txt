      calibration: Before we start: does the moon spin on its own axis? {Yes | No | Not sure}
      - b1 The claim that sounds wrong: The moon always shows Earth the same face, and the surprising reason is that it does spin, exactly once per orbit.
      - b2 Watch it orbit without spinning: A moon that circles Earth without rotating would show Earth every side of itself over one orbit, so a non-spinning moon cannot explain the fixed face.
      - b3 Spin once per lap: When the moon turns once on its axis in exactly the time it takes to orbit once, the same face stays pointed at Earth the whole way around.
      - b4 Relative to what?: From Earth the moon looks still, but from the distant stars it clearly turns once per orbit, so whether it spins depends on the viewpoint you measure from.
      - b5 Why the spin matches: Earth's gravity stretched the young moon into a slight bulge and tugged on it until its spin slowed to match its orbit, which is called tidal locking.
      - b6 Back to the hook: We see one side because the moon spins exactly once per orbit, locked there by tides, and the same thing happens to many moons.
