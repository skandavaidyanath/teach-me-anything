[  6s] HEADER "Where to sit in the circle of death" style=write-along topic=the Josephus problem: people in a circle, every second person is eliminated, where should you stand to be the last one left
      calibration: Before we start: have you heard of the Josephus problem? {Yes, I know the answer | I've heard of it | Never}
      - b1 Seven in a circle: With seven people in a circle, each killing the person to their left in turn, working it by hand shows that seat seven survives, which is not the seat most people guess.
      - b2 Gather the data: Working the small circles from one to eight people by hand gives a table of survivors that resets to seat one at one, two, four, and eight, and climbs by two in between.
      - b3 The pattern: Write the number of people as the largest power of two that fits plus a leftover L; the survivor is seat two times L plus one, which checks out on forty-one people as seat nineteen.
      - b4 Why powers of two reset: When the number of people is a power of two, each full lap halves the circle and hands the sword back to seat one, so seat one survives; with L extra people, after L kills the circle is a power of two and the person holding the sword, at seat two L plus one, is the survivor.
      - b5 The binary trick: In binary, the survivor's seat comes from moving the leading one of the count to the end, so forty-one, one zero one zero zero one, becomes one zero zero one one, which is nineteen.
