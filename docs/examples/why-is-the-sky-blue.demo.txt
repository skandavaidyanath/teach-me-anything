[  6s] HEADER "Why the sky is blue, and why sunsets are red" style=demo topic=why is the sky blue
      calibration: Quick check before we start: what do you think makes the sky blue? {It reflects the blue ocean | Air scatters blue light more than red | I have no idea}
      - b1 The ocean answer is wrong: Sunlight is white, a mix of every colour from red to violet, and the sky is blue even over deserts, so it is not reflecting the ocean.
      - b2 Watch air bounce light: When sunlight passes through air, tiny molecules knock some of it sideways, and that sideways light is mostly blue, because short wavelengths scatter far more than long ones.
      - b3 Look away from the sun: You see the sky's colour by looking away from the sun, so what reaches your eye from the sky is only the scattered light, and scattered light is blue.
      - b4 Why not violet?: Violet scatters even more than blue, but sunlight has less violet in it and your eyes are less sensitive to it, so the mix you see looks sky blue.
      - b5 Sunset: a longer path: At sunset the light crosses far more air, so most of the blue is scattered out before it reaches you, and the direct sunlight that is left is red and orange.
      - b6 Settling the hook: The sky is blue because air scatters short wavelengths much more strongly, roughly as one over wavelength to the fourth power, and the same rule makes sunsets red.
